System and method for analog matrix computation
using inverted meta-learning.
Technical Field
The invention relates to the field of computing hardware for artificial
intelligence, more specifically to a hybrid architecture for analog in-memory
computing ("In-Memory Computing"). The invention combines two analog
correction matrices and one analog primary computation matrix with a
digital control unit to form a joint hybrid atomic triad. These physical
substrates, consisting of multiple hybrid atomic matrix triads, execute metalearning algorithms to achieve digital determinism in a non-deterministic
analog medium.

Background of the Invention
Conventional digital systems for neural networks are strictly deterministic
but suffer from high power consumption due to the so-called "memory wall"
(data transfer between memory and processor). Analog computing systems
offer high energy efficiency but are limited by physical non-idealities such as
thermal drift, voltage leakage, and component variations.
Existing meta-learning (such as "MAML" Model-Agnostic Meta-Learning) is
primarily used to adapt digital models to new external tasks (referred to as
"few-shot learning"). There is currently a lack of systems that use intrinsic
("inward") meta-learning to compensate for internal variations in analog
computing hardware in real-time during operation.
One of the most limiting factors in today's digital AI systems is the so-called
Von Neumann bottleneck, often referred to as the 'memory wall'. In matrixvector multiplication, which constitutes the majority of computations in an
LLM, large amounts of data (weights) must constantly be moved between
the memory and the processor. The energy consumption for moving this
data is often several orders of magnitude higher than for the computation
itself. This makes large-scale AI energy-intensive and difficult to implement
in resource-constrained edge systems.

A pervasive problem for today's leading actors in analog in-memory
computing (IMC)—whether based on resistive elements (e.g., memristors) or
the charge domain (e.g., switched capacitors)—is the difficulty of
maintaining deterministic precision over time. To make analog cell
intersections function consistently, manufacturers are forced to rely on
tight, complex, and costly manufacturing tolerances in the fabrication
process for ASIC circuits. Furthermore, current IMC technology relies
primarily on static, pre-calibration (offline calibration) and hardware-aware
training (Hardware-Aware Training). This means attempting to fix the
system so that the analog cells remain deterministic throughout their entire
operating lifetime. However, this static approach fails to handle dynamic
fluctuations that occur during operation, such as thermal drift, temporary
voltage drops, or long-term component aging. Thus, there is a significant
and unfulfilled need for an architecture that breaks free from the
requirement of perfect physical manufacturing precision and static
determinism. There is a lack of a solution that instead allows the analog
hardware to be naturally imperfect, but which can dynamically and
autonomously adjust and compensate for these internal variations in realtime, without losing either the energy efficiency of the analog computation
or the reliability of digital systems.

Summary of the Invention
The core of the invention is an inverted MAML architecture. In conventional
MAML, a model is adapted to a changing environment, but here, a digital
reference model (a deterministic truth) is used instead to adapt an analog
computing core—a so-called "atomic triad" (3, 8, 33) representing a nondeterministic reality. The analog correction matrices (3, 8) and the
activation functions (4, 10) together constitute the inverted MAML
structure. The system thereby learns to continuously adjust, during
operation, the errors in the third analog matrix (33) using short windows for
correction measurements.
Through this architecture, a form of technical proprioception is introduced,
wherein the atomic computing cores are allowed to become aware of their
own local "body" and its physical state. In this context, proprioception is
defined as the system's ability to register and compensate for internal
physical parameters, such as thermal drift and voltage leakage, in real-time
via the reference nodes (29) and the meta-learning algorithm, without
external calibration data.
By leveraging the MAML algorithm's capacity for rapid adaptation via few
measurement points (few-shot learning), each computing core is arranged to
autonomously perform calibration during short temporal windows (20, 24).
This internal correction cycle operates at a lower frequency than the
primary computing path (payload), allowing local hardware deviations to be
compensated for in real-time without affecting the system's throughput or
latency during the inference phase.
Through an atomic triad structure, complex non-linearities are eliminated by
letting the primary matrix (33) perform the main computation while the
cascaded layers, the correction matrices (3, 8), add an inverted MAML
deviation correction at the summing point (35). The resulting output signal
(11) is controlled via feedback (13) to deterministically correspond to the
digital reference model (39).
To maintain precision in gradient extraction and avoid temporal blur
(integration losses) during the discharge phase (31), short temporal
windows (20, 24) are used. Instead of long measurement periods, the
measured values are downsampled by placing snapshot windows at exactly
the same phase position across multiple refresh cycles (18). This enables
high instantaneous precision even for large matrices without the information
being averaged over time within the measurement window.

The system is trained into a robust base model (40, 41) through an
emulation process corresponding to a changing hardware state during
operational inference (42). Instead of physical relocation of different
substrates, emulated operating variations for the power supply of the analog
matrices are injected via a controllable DAC (trim-DAC) (38). During base
model training (40, 41), small unknown offset values are also added for the
MAML training (40, 41) to weights, the DAC (2), and the ADCs (45, 49) to
emulate the physical deviations of the analog medium. By varying these
emulated errors during training, the base model is exposed to the total
statistical variations of the hardware population. The inverted MAML model
thus becomes proficient at rapidly re-trimming itself against variations
during operation. Upon transition to normal operational inference drift (42),
all emulated offset values are removed and said trim-DAC (38) is reset. This
creates a base model that, with a minimal number of samples and via an
inner calibration loop (41), can be fine-tuned to the natural state of the
specific physical core, even during ongoing inference (19) to the system's
output signal (11).
This adaptability is enabled by the deviation correction in the components
(3, 4, 8, 10) being specifically designed as a multi-layer non-linear
architecture. The purpose is to streamline the system's modeling capability
to exclusively map and invert underlying physical hardware errors, rather
than burdening the meta-learning algorithm with learning the primary
logical computation, which remains fixed in the primary matrix (33). By
separating the primary inference computation (payload computation)—
whose applied matrix weights are deterministic and fixed, but whose analog
output signal inevitably varies—from the adaptive non-linear error
correction, sufficiently accurate compensation is made possible (for
example, 6-bit determinism on the outgoing vector computation compared to
the computation of the ideal digital model). When the output signal of the
error correction is linearly summed with the fluctuating output from the
primary matrix at the summing point (35), the computational deviations are
neutralized, whereby the system achieves digital precision despite the
inherent variations of the analog medium.
By utilizing the proposed hybrid architecture (Fig. 1), the need to move
weights during the inference phase itself (19) is eliminated, as the weights
are physically stored in the analog matrix (3, 8, 33). A crucial aspect of the
invention is the asymmetry in memory bandwidth between computation and
control (Fig. 1).

While the analog matrices (3, 8, 33) perform numerous parallel
computations with high throughput, the digital control unit (9) operates with
a memory bandwidth that is orders of magnitude lower. This is possible
because the digital control unit (9) only needs to communicate the finetuned correction values (via the MAML algorithm) in a slow loop (12, 13, 14,
15, 16, 17) to maintain sufficiently high determinism. By separating the
high-frequency data throughput (analog) from the low-frequency calibration
(digital), a significant reduction in total system power is obtained without
losing precision.
An important decisive technical advantage of the invention is the functional
synergy between the rapid convergence of the MAML algorithm and the
analog hardware architecture's need for calibration during operation. The
system is arranged to operate with an optimized duty cycle (Fig. 5), where
the majority of the operation is dedicated to uninterrupted inference (19),
while a minimal fraction of the time—preferably on the order of a few
percent—is reserved for automatic fine-calibration. Since the inverted
MAML algorithm is specifically trained to achieve adaptive adjustment with
a minimal number of snapshot measurement readings (samples) (referred to
as 'few-shot learning'), a complete fine-tuning of the analog weights is made
possible within the short time windows (20, 24) available between the
matrix's refresh pulses (18). This symbiosis between algorithm and
hardware architecture means that the system can maintain maximum data
throughput while digital determinism is ensured through rapid, periodic
correction. This ability to interpolate and transition between different analog
states with few measurement points makes the combination of MAML and
the proposed hybrid matrix structure (Fig. 1, 2, 3, 4) uniquely suited for
demanding real-time AI applications.
As an integrated part of this resource efficiency optimization, the system
applies a time-distributed acquisition strategy for gradient extraction.
Instead of requiring an extensive and energy-consuming measurement
window in each individual cycle, the collection of measurement data from
the specific points in time (21, 22, 23) is spread out over multiple
subsequent refresh cycles (18).

Through this downsampled method, the digital control unit (9) can
successively build up a complete picture of the state of the matrix by
sampling different parts of the weight matrix at exactly the same relative
points in time during the process, but in different chronological cycles. This
allows for an asymmetric update where large amounts of analog weights can
be fine-tuned with a minimal digital bandwidth. This capability to perform
high-precision calibration via a distributed measurement series ensures that
the system maintains its determinism over time, without encroaching on the
real-time performance required for continuous inference (19).
To further optimize this calibration process and maximize the signal-to-noise
ratio (SNR), the system implements a parallel encoding unit (6) (excitation
device) based on orthogonal encoding, preferably Hadamard encoding (43)
(Fig. 9). By exciting all rows in the matrix simultaneously with unique,
orthogonal patterns (Fig. 9), the digital control unit (9) can isolate individual
cell deviations via a Fast Hadamard Transform (FHT). In contrast to a serial
sequence, where each row is analyzed in isolation (conventional sequential
row scanning), this parallel method allows the perturbation energy to be
distributed across all rows during the measurement cycle. This results in a
significant lowering of the noise floor and thus a higher precision in the
estimated gradients, without requiring an extension of the temporal
measurement window's duration. The method maintains the system's critical
charge neutrality across the weight capacitors (44), while adding a
statistical robustness that is necessary to ensure determinism in large-scale
matrices where thermal noise would otherwise limit calibration accuracy.
To ensure maximum correlation between reference measurements and
actual computational processes, the matrix is arranged such that the
dedicated reference cells (29) (according to claim 2) are physically
interleaved with the active computing cells (27) for both the first (3) and
second (8) layers. This complete interdigitation (30) means that the
reference cells (29) are exposed to identical local thermal fluctuations and
process variations as the active cells, which eliminates measurement errors
caused by spatial distances on the semiconductor substrate.
Through the proposed hybrid architecture (Fig. 1), the need for complex and
time-consuming simulations of the analog hardware is eliminated. Since the
system, via the digital control unit (9), adapts to the actual physical
variations in real-time, the requirement to create precise deterministic
models of the analog medium is bypassed.

System Architecture and Signal Flow.
To realize the inverted MAML architecture, the hardware is arranged in a
specific sequential chain that enables both high-performance inference (19)
and precise gradient extraction. A digital input signal (1) is converted in the
DAC (2) and fed into the first analog correction matrix (3) (Layer 1), the
output signal of which passes through a first activation function (4) (analog
or digital). This signal is then fed into the second analog correction matrix
(8) (Layer 2), followed by an activation function (10), creating an internal
deviation correction signal up to the summer (35). In parallel, the main
computation is performed from the input signal (2) into the analog matrix
(33). The output signal from the primary matrix (33) is summed with the
output signal from the activation function (10), which is then converted at
full speed in an ADC (49) to create the digital output signal (11).

This atomic triad of matrices (Fig. 1) is arranged for continuous state
monitoring via a digital control unit (9). To enable differential perturbation
analysis with a high signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) without affecting the
integrity of the stored analog weights, the system includes a modulated
perturbation source (5). (VPTU). The perturbation source is controlled by the
control unit (9) and is connected to an encoding unit (6) (Hadamard
encoder). Unlike a conventional selector, the encoding unit (6) is arranged
to simultaneously inject controlled perturbation signals onto all row
conductors (PL) in matrix (3) or (8). This injection takes place in accordance
with an orthogonal coding scheme (Fig. 9) (43), where each individual row is
assigned a unique pattern of phase positions (positive or negative polarity)
over a measurement cycle. The injection occurs via dedicated perturbation
capacitances CPTU (7) whose capacitance values are chosen to be orders of
magnitude lower than the actual storage capacitance of the weights CW (44).
This relationship, where CPTU (7) < CW (44), ensures that the encoded
perturbation signals can modulate the row signals sufficiently for digital
detection at the output, while the remaining charge (the weight) in the
matrix remains unaffected. By utilizing orthogonal encoding, the control unit
(9) can mathematically separate the summed output signal into individual
partial derivatives

(∂)

for each cell, even though all rows are excited simultaneously during the
measurement window.
To maintain the charge of the weights over time and enable the iterative,
inverted meta-learning adjustment, the system includes an addressing
device (12, 15, 34). This configuration allows the control unit (9) to update
weight values (27, 29) in the matrices based on the perturbation analysis
extracted during the short measurement window (20), without interrupting
the majority of the time during the discharge phase or disturbing the main
high-frequency signal flow (payload) during inference (19). It should be
noted that the primary computation matrix (33) does not include said
perturbation signal mechanism, since the weights in the matrix (33) are
exclusively redistributed (reallocated) but never adjusted in substance.
Thereby, the need for perturbation analysis for this specific primary matrix
(33) is eliminated.

The activation circuits (4, 10) and the summer (35) preferably consist of
analog circuits to minimize energy consumption and complexity, but may
alternatively be digital.
By utilizing analog operational amplifiers for the activation functions (4) and
(10), a natural, smooth S-curve is obtained completely without the need for
complex digital computational logic. Since the inverted MAML architecture
provides continuous self-calibration, the inherent problems of component
drift and unstable curve shapes are eliminated, which allows the analog
saturation curve to be used as a stable and precise mathematical operator.
The control unit (9) is arranged to continuously compare the system's digital
output signal (11, 13) with a digital reference model, which represents the
deterministic truth. Hereby, the system is calibrated against the actual
physical reality rather than against a theoretical simulation model, ensuring
determinism even under changing environmental conditions such as
fluctuating temperature.
In a preferred embodiment of the invention, the matrix unit (3) comprises at
least one temporal reference cell (29). In contrast to the computing cells
(27) of the matrix, which are optimized to maintain an electrical charge (31)
over time, the temporal reference cell (29) is configured with a dedicated
and accelerated discharge path (28). In one embodiment, this discharge
path (28) may consist of a resistive element arranged in parallel with the
storage capacitance of the cell, or in an alternative embodiment, of an
actively biased transistor functioning as a constant-current source for
controlled leakage.
The purpose of the discharge path (28) is to generate a discharge curve (32)
with a steepness that substantially exceeds the natural voltage leakage (31)
of the computing cells (27). By dimensioning the discharge path (28) such
that the voltage drop (32) of the temporal reference cell covers a substantial
part of, and preferably the entirety of, the available analog signal range (Full
Swing) between two consecutive measurement windows (18), a physical
normalization of the time axis is achieved.
This configuration maximizes the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) during the
digital control unit's reading, as the time information is correlated (mapped)
against the full dynamic range of the matrix. The digital control unit can
thereby, with high precision, use the curve (32) as an analog timestamp to
interpolate and compensate for the significantly slower drift in the
computing cells of the matrix via the inverted meta-learning algorithm.

The analog computing cores (30, 37) (Fig. 2), (Fig. 3) are constructed as a
crossbar matrix where the input signals are injected via a set of input
buffers (26). These buffers, preferably implemented as high-impedance
operational amplifiers, ensure that the voltage signal $V_{in}$ is driven
stably across the rows of the matrix regardless of the combined resistive
load from the programmable weights. By utilizing these precision buffers
(26), initial signal errors are minimized already at the input stage, which
provides the MAML algorithm with a more linear base to work from.
The system's matrix architecture is defined by two internal configurations: a
reference structure (30) (Fig. 2) comprising discharge cells (29), and a
computing structure (37) (Fig. 3) without discharge cells. The first matrix (3)
is implemented as a hybrid structure where an upper part comprises the
reference structure (30) and a lower part comprises the computing structure
(37). The second matrix (8) and the third matrix (33) are, in their entirety,
uniformly constructed according to the computing structure (37). Because
the matrices (3, 8, 33) are arranged in thermal cooperation, the discharge
cells (29) in the first matrix (3) are utilized as a common reference for the
calibration of all matrices, allowing matrix (8) and (33) to be optimized for
maximum computational area without internal reference cells.

I. The system's procedure for gradient extraction is performed in a
sequential order as follows:

A digital input signal (1) is fed via the DAC (2) through the first matrix (3)
and the second matrix (8) for "inverted" MAML. The input signal (1) via the
DAC (2) is also fed through the primary computation matrix (33), which has
been loaded with fixed, deterministic weights (34) represented from the
digital ideal original model (39). The output signals from the activation
function (10) are summed with the output signals from the primary matrix
(33). The control unit (9) reads (samples) a reference value at the
intermediate ADC (45) as well as the resulting error at the final output (11,
13).
The perturbation analysis takes place in two separate steps for the weights
in matrix (3) and matrix (8) respectively. The measurement is done
internally with the ADC (45) between Layer 1 and Layer 2. This is necessary
to correctly calculate the chain rule digitally, and to avoid "spaghetti" mixed
columns and rows between matrix (3) and matrix (8) in the perturbation
analysis.
Analysis of the Chain Rule and Total Loss for Matrix (8):
To extract the local derivative for an individual cell (weight) in matrix (8) in
relation to the total loss function (Total Loss), the control unit (9) applies a
hardware-assisted calculation of the chain rule.
When the encoding unit (6) is active, a known perturbation VPTU(5) is created
on (PL-LINE). A momentary dynamic change (AC delta) then occurs, which
propagates to the output. The control unit measures this AC delta at each
column after the activation function (10).

precision and system utilization:
1. Data Collection (Excitation Phase): During a defined temporal

the internal ADC (45). Once the measurement series for the orthogonal
sequence is complete, the measurement window (20) closes.

window closes, the digital control unit (9) processes the stored measurement
values with a Fast Hadamard Transform (FHT). This mathematical decoding
separates the summed signal into individual partial derivatives

compromising the system's real-time performance or latency.

II. Calibration cycle, Meta-adaptation:

With the stored derivatives from both correction matrices (3, 8), the control
unit (9) performs a meta-learning-based update (40, 41) of the system's
control parameters to minimize the global error at the output (11).
Detailed description: hybrid architecture with triple matrices and non-linear
deviation correction The present invention utilizes an architecture that
separates the logical computation from the physical hardware compensation
through three interacting matrix units: The primary computation matrix
(33):
This analog matrix is loaded with frozen, deterministic weights (34) from a
digital source model (39) (e.g., an LLM). This unit performs the main
mathematical operation but is subject to chip-specific physical non-idealities.

The dual MAML correction branch, the correction matrices (3, 8):
To handle complex, non-linear errors (such as XOR-like dependencies and Sshaped saturation curves), the correction branch comprises at least two
serial matrix layers with intermediate non-linear activation functions. First
stage (3): Projects the input signal (2) to a hidden space via a first correction
matrix (3) and activation function (4). Second stage (8): Transforms the
hidden representation via the correction matrix (8) and activation function
(10) to generate a higher-order correction vector.
The output from the correction branch (from matrix 8) is routed to an analog
activation function (10). Thereafter, the output signal from the analog
activation function (10) is linearly summed with the output from the primary
computation matrix (33) via an adder (35). Said adder (35) is preferably an
analog summing circuit configured to maintain the signal processing entirely
in the analog domain up to the summation point. This combined analog
signal vector then undergoes a final analog-to-digital conversion (ADC),
which ensures that the entire computation block delivers a digital result that
corresponds to an ideal digital multiplication matrix.

Base model training via isolated hardware emulation (Fig 7).
According to one embodiment, the system is trained into a robust base
model through a method based on controlled hardware emulation, wherein
the physical hardware substrates (the atomic cores) are kept completely
isolated from each other during the entire process. In contrast to methods
based on spatial permutation or relocation of logical weights across separate
substrates, each individual inverted MAML atom is arranged to exclusively
operate with its own dedicated hybrid atomic triad consisting of the analog
matrices (3, 8, 33).
To expose the base model to a sufficient search space during training, a
dynamic emulation of thermal drift, voltage deviations, and altered
discharge characteristics is introduced. In the outer loop (40) of the base
model training, it is emulated that the three analog matrices (3, 8, 33)
rapidly change their physical state. This emulates environmental effects
such as heating or cooling of the hardware, accelerated or retarded
discharge rates, or that the supply voltage VDD (36) has drifted from its
nominal value.
To emulate voltage drift, the hardware architecture comprises a dedicated
trim-DAC (38), which is arranged to be able to shift the supply voltage V DD
(36) within a predetermined interval, preferably in the order of magnitude of
±5%. The remaining part of the state emulation is realized programmatically
via the digital control unit (9) by intentionally adding hidden offset values to
the matrix weights

W (3,8,33) ​i , j

, as well as hidden, static digital offset

values on the input signal's DAC (2) and the signal paths' ADC (45) and ADC
(49). These offsets are applied without the inverted MAML algorithm having
knowledge of or direct access to the actual offset values.
The digital control unit (9) is arranged to apply these hidden state shifts
according to the following specific mechanisms:
1. Column-wise gain deviation: A joint offset is applied across an entire
column of matrix weights, which for the inverted MAML algorithm emulates
that the analog gain for the column in question has increased or decreased.
2. Time-based discharge deviation: An unknown offset is applied to the
discharge time of the bias cells (29), which misleads the inverted MAML
algorithm into interpreting the hardware state as though the discharge
phase has progressed faster or slower than normal.

3. Input deviation (offset) and leakage emulation (DAC 2): A hidden static
offset addition is applied to the digital input vector before it is converted by
the DAC (2), which emulates a physical input offset voltage in the driver
stages or constant electrical current leakage on the input lines.
4. Converter deviation and non-linear distortion (ADC 45 and ADC 49): By
adding or subtracting fixed digital values after the analog-to-digital
conversion in ADC (45) and ADC (49), DC offsets, thermal zero-point errors,
or dark currents in the signal path's amplifier stages are emulated. This
intentionally shifts the operating point of the signal in relation to the nonlinear activation functions (4, 10), forcing the inverted MAML algorithm to
develop a proprioceptive ability to handle and calibrate away complex nonlinear hardware skewness.
These artificial variations are generated by the digital control unit (9)
secretly adding the hidden offset pattern across selected weights and
converter registers. This unique pattern is then kept strictly fixed during the
execution of the MAML algorithm's inner loop (41) in order to emulate a
stable but shifted hardware state.
To ensure that the base model becomes robust and is not overfitted to static
input signals during this fixed operating state, the system is arranged to
permute and vary a multiplicity of different incoming digital vectors via the
DAC (2) during the inner loop (41). Through this procedure, the underlying
characteristic pattern for a specific hardware individual is maintained, while
its virtual state location is displaced during the base model training to
correspond to normal field variations, thereby establishing an optimal
search space for the algorithm.
Upon transitioning to operational proprioception and normal operation in
the inner loop (42), the digital control unit (9) is arranged and configured to
completely remove all emulated and hidden offset additions from weights

W (3,8,33) ​i , j ,DAC (2), ADC (45), and ADC (49). Simultaneously, the trimDAC (38) is reset to its nominal center position. The system thereafter
operates under its natural hardware conditions, whereby the real-time
operating inner calibration loop fine-tunes the correction matrices (3, 8)
against the actual physical substrate to counteract instantaneous physical
drift. Fig 12 illustrates a differential implementation of the analog matrix
comprising dual 2T1C cells for a differential multiplier. To establish a virtual
zero point, a differential mirror structure according to industry standard is
utilized, where the synaptic weight is represented by a positive weight
component (W+) and a negative weight component (W-).

Fig 12 specifically illustrates how the system's row perturbation (for
example, a Hadamard perturbation) is integrated into this environment. To
optimize the perturbation response, the system is arranged to apply the
perturbation signal in a differential and inverted manner across this
structure. More specifically, the perturbation injection comprises two
separate perturbation rows; a first positive perturbation row applied to the
positive weight component (W+) and a corresponding inverted (negative)
perturbation row applied to the negative weight component (W-). This
configuration of differential and inverted perturbation rows ensures that the
resulting transient signal can be isolated and evaluated with high precision
across the differential structure, with minimized impact from common-mode
noise. It should be noted that the use of the weight components (W+, W-)
and the mirrored structure constitutes an industry standard for establishing
said virtual zero point. Since individual multiplier transistors can only
conduct current in one direction, the virtual zero point is intentionally raised
from absolute zero current in the column. Through the implementation of a
mirrored dual column, a differential output signal is thus generated that is
essentially insensitive to offsets from the virtual midpoint.

Abstract
The invention relates to a hybrid computation architecture for neural
networks comprising analog matrices and a digital control unit. The
architecture utilizes an inverted meta-learning algorithm configured for
internal parametric adaptation, wherein the algorithm is arranged to
estimate and compensate for internal hardware variations rather than
external input characteristics.
Through atomic triads of interleaved analog matrices and the use of short
temporal measurement windows for differential perturbation analysis, the
architecture achieves a deterministic stability independent of thermal drift
or component aging. The system is arranged to asymmetrically separate a
primary high-frequency computation path (payload) from a low-frequency
calibration cycle, enabling analog computation with digital precision at a
reduced energy consumption. The architecture is scalable for both cloudbased computing systems and low-power edge computing systems.
